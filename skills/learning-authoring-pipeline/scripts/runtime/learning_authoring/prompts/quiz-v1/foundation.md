# Quiz foundation

Author questions a teacher could use to find out what a learner can actually do with the supplied
knowledge. A question is useful when success depends on its target capability, not on recognizing
the wording of a definition, a conspicuous option, or a completed solution in the stem. Recall is
valid when recall is the intended target; do not disguise it as application.

An assessment slot names one bounded evidence target within a Leaf KC. Variants are alternate
instances of that same target, not different capabilities or cosmetic rewrites. Choose counts from
the supported learning needs, not a per-KC multiplier or a desired distribution of question types.

Choose the response that exposes the target work. A selection, relationship set or sequence can
be sufficient evidence. When the target is producing a solution rather than recognizing one,
require the learner to construct that work. Neither prose nor a complex topic makes a question
higher-level. Set Bloom and estimated difficulty
from the work left in the final unhinted question. Difficulty remains uncalibrated.

Bloom identifies the operation: remember retrieves/recognizes; understand interprets/explains
meaning; apply uses a procedure or knowledge; analyze identifies parts and relationships; evaluate
judges against criteria/evidence; create produces a coherent new solution under constraints.
Difficulty is separate and relative to the intended learners: easy is familiar with few interacting
decisions; medium requires meaningful coordination of conditions; hard demands substantial transfer,
integration or judgment. Unknown means unsupported estimation, not a fourth difficulty level.
Neither a high Bloom label nor a later position in a sequence implies hard.

A demanding learning goal may benefit from preparatory questions, guided practice, useful variants
and a fresh independent check. Choose what the learner needs, not a fixed ladder or hard-item
multiplier. Preparatory questions may have their own targets/KCs; do not merge them to fit one slot.
Scaffolds and hints can teach, but the same case solved afterward is not independent evidence.

The source is author context, not automatically visible to the learner. Supply the case, code,
table, formula, convention or image needed for the task. A citation and a hint cannot substitute
for missing initial data. Keep necessary facts while leaving the assessed inference unresolved.

Scoring describes the requested work, not one preferred phrasing. Accept other valid solutions;
reject answers that sound right but violate a necessary condition. Hints support a next step
without performing the assessed work. No safe helpful hint is a valid choice.

These are authoring criteria, not a guarantee of validity. Contract and form checks do not approve
questions or establish learner mastery.
